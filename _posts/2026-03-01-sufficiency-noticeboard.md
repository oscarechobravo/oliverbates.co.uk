Design provocations for futures of sustainability in scientific computing and digital research infrastructure. 

The Sufficiency Noticeboard was produced as part of the “Reimagining digital research infrastructure in environmental science for a sustainable future” funded by the UKRI EPSRC (EP/Z533744/1)

The Sufficiency Noticeboard is a speculative
design that asks “what might digital sufficiency
look like in the actions of DRI organisations and
communities in the future?”

As part of work delivering design futures work on the Sustainable Digital Research Infrastructure project I designed a noticeboard from the future. 

The aim was to produce a diegetic artefact to be deployed in everyday contexts with research organisations supporting or delivering environmental modelling and utilising scientific computing.

This is to provoke discussion about what a sufficient future might look like, and what might need to change in organisational structures and cultures whilst journeying towards that future. 

Workshop told us about the technology, but the big assumption was that an organisation would know what transformation towards a sufficient organisation looks like. 

In a recent project on digital sufficiency I produced a fictional noticeboard to explore how organisations and communities might look and feel whilst responding to shifts in practice towards digital sufficiency. 

an invitation to everyone to turn mundane everyday objects in our lives into objects that encourage thinking, being and acting that encourages the emergence of new and sustainable practices and actions.    

Noticeboards bring people together. They are located in throughout workplaces. Containing vital information. 
STICKY

Could they contain signals from the future? What could change today if we engage with signals from possible futures? How might our practices, connections, and communities change?

# Exploring Digital Sufficiency through the Noticeboard

- Pulling at themes in the data, reaching past them
- The form - some kind of mundane real world objects - thinking about existing office infrastructure/stuff - what is in office spaces, that we take for granted
- Noticeboards where I work - Where I work there are two distinct noticeboards. One in the kitchen and one in the corridor. When I think about it though, people put flyers in all kinds of places - on doors, windows, walls and wherever they think  a notice might be gazed upon by an interested party. 
- how long has that been there? I’m not sure how long any of these posters or flyers have been on noticeboards. Some are showing signs of aging, rough edges and the sun bleaching the colour out of them. The content is about a range of things user studies (I work in a computer science department), new institutional policies, job postings, advertisements for services, meet ups, wellbeing events, community groups and a whole host of other activities or areas of interest.
- Noticeboards as political assets - In some spaces noticeboards are political assets. Controlled by an office administrator. A site of tension where politics is front and centre. 
- Noticeboards are rich with history and texture. The repurposed wipeboard with smudges and pen marks. The corkboard where there are the staples and torn paper remains from flyers of the past. The history and stories of marks and pinholes left behind, the ripped corners of posts gone by
- loads of good thoughts and observations

# Reflections
## Something about sticky objects - the noticeboard themselves, also digital technologies and data objects that are sticky
Emotional stickiness of Noticeboards - (ahmed, 2014 - The Cultural Politics of Emotion) - affect
happy objects sara ahmed - affect as "sticky" - "Affect
is what sticks, or what sustains or preserves the connection
between ideas, values, and objects."

> "The circulation of objects is thus the circulation of goods. Objects are sticky because they are already attributed as being good or bad, as being the cause of happiness or unhappiness. This is why the social bond is always rather sensational. Groups cohere around a shared orientation toward some things as being good, treating some things and not others as the cause of delight. If the same objects make us happy-or if we invest in the same objects as being what should make us happy-then we would be orientated or directed in the same way. Consider that the word "promise" comes from the Latin *promissum* "to send forth:' The promise of happiness is what sends happiness forth; it is what allows happiness to be out and about. Happy objects are passed around, accumulating positive affective value as social goods. "

This whole paragraph is super interesting in the context of transformation, whether voluntary or forced, because as individuals and collectives, the affect, or stickiness between objects, ideas and values is going to be a challenging barrier to overcome due to how digital technologies and tools as objects are part of the connection to the work of environmental science, of doing good, of professional identity. 

*Do workers feel happy or unhappy if they are denied a hardware refresh?* *how are workers going to feel about losing parts of their work?*
